Work order page · proposal

Giving every fact on the work order one home

The page isn't messy because it has too much on it — it's messy because it has two competing organisations at once: tabs on the left, a tower of cards on the right, and the same facts living in both plus a raw field dump. Below: what's actually there today, three ways to reorganise it, and a map that gives all 61 fields a place.

Trade-offs

Easy to explain: "everything about money is under Money".

Each tab can use the full width — the Site tab gets a proper map, Money gets the payables table.

Eight tabs, and three of them (People, Site, Dates) hold five lines each. A tab for three names feels empty and costs a click.

The things you need while working — NTE headroom, who the AM is, when the SLA is due — disappear behind tabs. Today's rail, for all its clutter, kept them in view.

"All fields" survives as the junk drawer, so the duplication isn't solved.

Why this one

Every fact has one home: the rail shows a one-line summary of each area and links to it. No more card-and-dump duplication.

The rail never scrolls out of view: money headroom, people, site, next date and flags are always one glance away, on every tab.

Details replaces "All fields": the same 61 fields, but grouped into Site · People · Dates & SLA · Parts · Quality · Links · Admin, with human labels. The sections are the map below.

Four tabs is a number people remember. History = today's Audit trail, renamed for what it is.

Details is a long page (seven sections). It needs a section strip at the top (Site · People · Dates …) that scrolls you down — the wireframe for it is next.

Moves the AM into the header chip — a small header change.

Trade-offs

Nothing is hidden; Ctrl-F and printing just work; the sticky index doubles as a table of contents.

No "which tab was that on?" — good for people who open twenty work orders a day.

Long. With the activity feed and messages inline, the page becomes a scroll, and Messages (a live conversation) doesn't sit well between static sections.

The full-width sections waste the right third of a wide screen; the rail in B uses it.

Suggested decision

Option B. Four tabs — Overview · Details · Money · Messages · History — with an always-visible "At a glance" rail, the AM in the header, and "All fields" replaced by the sectioned Details tab using the map above.

  1. Decide the map first — especially the strike-throughs and the "Days since…" counters. Everything else follows from it.
  2. Step 1 (no data change): tabs + rail + Details sections, reading the same fields bag with friendlier labels.
  3. Step 2: make sections editable one at a time (Site, People, Dates), and retire the ClickUp numbering from the field names in Admin › Fields.
